<span style="font-weight: 400;">“Don’t worry, I take the PATH train all the time,” I said confidently to my sister, Misty, and brother-in-law, Travis. </span>

<span style="font-weight: 400;">We needed some positivity after the evening’s events. Our flight into Newark International Airport had been delayed by 2 hours, and we landed after 1 AM - right after the rental car counter had closed.</span>

<span style="font-weight: 400;">Our plan was to go to New Jersey for the holidays, but first we’d take a mini vacay to New York City before <em>facing</em> our family. We rotated holding my 3-month-old niece, Andie, in her carrier. This was her first trip to the east coast.</span>

<span style="font-weight: 400;">I grew up in New Jersey and would often go out with my friends in Hoboken, then take the PATH train to New York City to do </span><i><span style="font-weight: 400;">god knows what</span></i><span style="font-weight: 400;">. Misty had already paid for the rental car, so I didn’t want to have to pay a ton of money for a cab into Manhattan. </span>

<span style="font-weight: 400;">I could get us into the city, I thought. I mean, how different could it be? <em>The PATH is the PATH, right?</em></span>

<span style="font-weight: 400;">Misty and Travis were reluctant, but they followed my lead. We needed to take a bus from the airport to the PATH station. After talking to a few different airport employees, we made it onto the bus.</span><span style="font-weight: 400;">The first thing I noticed was that the bus driver was <em>fully enclosed</em> in bulletproof glass. There was a “no guns” sign displayed prominently.</span>

<span style="font-weight: 400;">The second thing I noticed was that there were no children. Everyone was staring at us.</span>

<span style="font-weight: 400;">Our bus ride was steely silent. By contrast, the train station we arrived to was complete chaos. </span>

<span style="font-weight: 400;">People lined the sidewalk along the train station, settling in for the night. Some people were talking loudly and playing games. Others were scantily dressed and sauntering up and down the sidewalk, stopping to talk to different men.</span>

<span style="font-weight: 400;"><strong>I’ll never forget the woman wearing the tube top</strong>. It had fallen down to reveal one of her breasts and she was screaming with her eyes rolling in the back of her head.</span>

<span style="font-weight: 400;">Misty had her 3-month old Andie hidden </span><i><span style="font-weight: 400;">under </span></i><span style="font-weight: 400;">her jacket. Travis was carrying the stroller and a bag. I was carrying the other bags. </span>

<span style="font-weight: 400;">We walked into the train station.</span>

<span style="font-weight: 400;">Inside there were even more people lining the walls. Some were screaming or mumbling. Others were staring at us.</span>

<span style="font-weight: 400;">There were no children. <em><strong>Anywhere</strong></em>.</span><span style="font-weight: 400;">I saw two cops standing in the center of the station, their eyes darting around to look at everything while at the same time looking at nothing at all.</span>

<span style="font-weight: 400;">We walked toward them quick, but tried to act casual. <strong>They wouldn’t look at us. </strong>Their eyes just kept darting around.</span>

<span style="font-weight: 400;">“<em>What are you </em></span><em><span style="font-weight: 400;">doing</span></em><span style="font-weight: 400;"><em> here</em>,” one cop whispered harshly through strained teeth, still never making eye contact with us. The way he said it, it was more a statement than a question.</span>

<span style="font-weight: 400;">We mumbled something unmemorable.</span>

<span style="font-weight: 400;">Still without ever making eye contact, the cops escorted us to a train platform entrance that had been previously locked.</span>

<span style="font-weight: 400;">“Get on the train when it comes,” one curtly said as he unlocked it. “<em>Don’t talk to anyone</em>.” </span>

<span style="font-weight: 400;">We wait.</span><span style="font-weight: 400;">At this point my sister was whispering to Andie, “<em>Please don’t cry</em>. I’m the worst mother. I’m the worst mother, please don’t cry.”</span>

<span style="font-weight: 400;"><strong>Travis was pale and hadn’t said a word the whole time</strong>. He had his tough guy face on.</span>

<span style="font-weight: 400;">I'm cursing myself for such a stupid idea, wondering where the hell the train was. I watched nervously as two people halfway down the platform stared at us whispering. </span>

<i><span style="font-weight: 400;">Please stay over there</span></i><span style="font-weight: 400;">, I thought to myself.</span>

<span style="font-weight: 400;">The train finally came. We got on and took the empty train to Manhattan without a hitch. </span>

<span style="font-weight: 400;">But when we get to the subway entrance, the stroller didn’t fit through the turnstile.</span>

<span style="font-weight: 400;">Travis, silent up to this point, flipped his $hit and throws the stroller against the subway station wall.</span>

<span style="font-weight: 400;">It's 4 AM so the subway station was extremely silent, except for the echo from the impact reverberating through the space.</span>

<span style="font-weight: 400;">Then out of nowhere, a woman came <em>flying</em> out of a two-way mirrored room we hadn't noticed before, screaming at us.</span>

<span style="font-weight: 400;">"What the f&amp;ck do you think you're doing?! <strong>Who the f&amp;ck are you?!</strong>"</span>

<span style="font-weight: 400;">We ignored her and walked on the subway train that was waiting there. It’s waiting for more passengers. Our anger was palatable, but it was late, our energy zapped. </span>

<span style="font-weight: 400;">Suddenly two cops came onto the train asking us what's going on.</span>

<span style="font-weight: 400;">I was silent and avoided eye contact. Travis was also silent, looking down. My sister started crying and said something unintelligible about her baby and being a terrible mother.</span>

<span style="font-weight: 400;">They leave.</span>

<em><span style="font-weight: 400;">The train leaves.</span></em><span style="font-weight: 400;">It was just about 5 AM when we stepped onto the New York City sidewalk. <em>Of course it was snowing.</em></span>

<span style="font-weight: 400;">There were no cars on the street, so a cab wasn’t an option. Resolved to get to bed, <strong>we walked 20 blocks to the hotel in the snow</strong>.</span>

<span style="font-weight: 400;">At this point, I don't think we even had the stroller anymore.</span>

<span style="font-weight: 400;">Of course, Travis’ suitcase handle broke off about 10 blocks in. He had to hold the suitcase the rest of the way.</span>

<span style="font-weight: 400;">It’s 6 AM when we finally collapse into bed with the hair dryer running because there wasn't a fan and that's how my niece fell asleep.</span>

<span style="font-weight: 400;">We were terrified at the time, but now <strong>Misty, Travis, and I think back on that story and <em>laugh</em></strong>. We lost a bit of time in New York City because we slept in most of that first day, but the rest of the trip was actually a ton of fun. </span>

<span style="font-weight: 400;">Still, it taught me a few important lessons.</span>

<span style="color: #ff0000;"><strong>Here is what I learned*</strong></span>
<ul>
 	<li style="font-weight: 400;"><span style="font-weight: 400;">The PATH station in Newark closes at night. Because of this, we were never actually at the PATH station. We were at the <em>Newark Train Station</em>.</span></li>
 	<li style="font-weight: 400;"><span style="font-weight: 400;">In the evening, the Newark Train Station isn’t like the PATH station I used to take from Hoboken, NJ into the city.</span></li>
 	<li style="font-weight: 400;"><span style="font-weight: 400;">Because of this, people don't go there with children.</span></li>
 	<li style="font-weight: 400;"><span style="font-weight: 400;">Correction, <em>nobody</em> goes there unless they plan to spend the night.</span></li>
 	<li style="font-weight: 400;"><span style="font-weight: 400;">I'm 100% convinced that the only reason we didn't get hassled or possibly mugged is because my niece was there.</span></li>
 	<li style="font-weight: 400;"><span style="font-weight: 400;">Babies have an uncanny ability to sense fear and be quiet when needed.</span></li>
 	<li style="font-weight: 400;"><span style="font-weight: 400;">Don't vandalize subway stations...even if it's 4AM and it seems nobody is around.</span></li>
</ul>
