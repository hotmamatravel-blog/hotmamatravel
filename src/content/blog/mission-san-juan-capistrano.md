<span style="color: #000000;">Founded in 1775, Mission San Juan Capistrano is steeped in history, culture and religion. As the intention of all the California Missions, San Juan Capistrano was established to expand the Spanish territory by teaching natives the ways of European life and converting them to Catholicism. </span>

<span style="color: #000000;">Once baptized, natives were integrated into the mission community. This meant that they were not to leave the property of the mission without permission and had to make many lifestyle changes such as learning a new language, new religion, skills and social customs. </span>

<span style="color: #000000;">For years Mission San Juan Capistrano thrived, but when Mexico won their Independence from Spain, the Mexican government ended the missions and sold them off. San Juan Capistrano served as a private ranch home before the U.S won the Mexican American War. </span>

<span style="color: #000000;">Once California was given to the U.S and declared a state, President Abraham Lincoln returned the missions to the church and restoration began. All this history can be seen, touched, experienced and preserved by simply making a trip to visit a California Mission. So pack up the kids, because today we are checking out the gorgeous Mission San Juan Capistrano with kids!</span>

<h2><span style="color: #000000;"><em><strong>Money Saving Tips:</strong></em></span></h2>
<span style="color: #000000;">What I love about visiting missions is that they are usually inexpensive and the money received goes to maintaining the historical site. With low ticket prices, San Juan Capistrano makes for a perfect day trip for the family and is one of our <strong><span style="color: #ff0000;"><a href="/orange-county-family-travel/" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer"><span style="color: #ff0000;">top weekend destinations in Orange County</span></a></span></strong>.</span>
<h3><span style="color: #000000;"><strong>Admission:</strong> </span></h3>
<span style="color: #000000;">Included in the cost of admission, you will receive a free audio tour with detailed accounts of various locations around the mission.</span>
<ul>
 	<li><span style="color: #000000;">Adults are $9</span></li>
 	<li><span style="color: #000000;">Seniors (60+) are $8</span></li>
 	<li><span style="color: #000000;">Kids (4-11) are $6</span></li>
 	<li><span style="color: #000000;">Kids under 3 are FREE!</span></li>
 	<li><span style="color: #000000;">4th graders get a special deal and be sure to check if there is a special event or concert going on that could change the admission price.</span></li>
</ul>
<h3><span style="color: #000000;"><strong>Donations:</strong> </span></h3>
<span style="color: #000000;">In various rooms throughout the mission you will notice donation boxes. They are optional and the money goes towards preserving the mission.</span>
<h3><span style="color: #000000;"><strong>Shop:</strong> </span></h3>
<span style="color: #000000;">Not only a great place to find fun souvenirs, but also a way to give back to the mission. Net proceeds from the store help educational programs and restoration projects for the mission. So, buy away. Our visit comes days before Father Serra is to be sainted by the Pope Francis, so I bought everyone Pope Francis and San Serra metals. A nice reminder of our day and the time in history we visited.</span>
<h2><em><span style="color: #000000;"><strong>Fun Things to do in Mission San Juan Capistrano:</strong></span></em></h2>
<span style="color: #000000;">The last time I visited San Juan Capistrano was years ago, before I even had babies. I used to love to wandering the historical grounds and enjoy the fantastic nearby restaurants. Recently, I was feeling the need to be around something historical, a ghost town or old town of some kind. Then it hit me! I had never taken the kids to Mission San Juan Capistrano. Bingo!</span>

<span style="color: #000000;"><a style="color: #000000;" href="/mission-san-juan-capistrano/"><img class="aligncenter wp-image-3280" title="Kids at Mission San Juan Capistrano" src="https://hotmamatravel.com/wp-content/uploads/2015/09/DSCN0822-e1492840599818-1024x935.jpg" alt="Kids at Mission San Juan Capistrano" width="640" height="584" /></a></span>

<span style="color: #000000;">The property is just gorgeous with fountains and gardens to explore. They make for some pretty awesome photo ops as well. I took the opportunity to recreate a photo I had taken with HotDaddy years before, this time with our happy babies. I’m a dork and love doing recreation photos of past travels, so you’ll see a lot of them on my blog.</span>

<a href="/mission-san-juan-capistrano/" rel="attachment wp-att-5423"><img class="aligncenter wp-image-5423" title="Family at San Juan Capistrano" src="https://hotmamatravel.com/wp-content/uploads/2015/09/SanJuan-1024x512.jpg" alt="Family at San Juan Capistrano" width="640" height="320" /></a>
<h3><span style="color: #000000;"> The Fountains </span></h3>
<span style="color: #000000;">Covered with gorgeous lily pads are giant Koi hiding below. My kids found this very fascinating and really the highlight of their day. My little mermaid, FireCracker, seemed to have a real connection the fish as they would gather near her and poke their heads to the surface for a fishy kiss.</span>

<a href="/mission-san-juan-capistrano/" rel="attachment wp-att-5424"><img class="aligncenter wp-image-5424" title="Mission San Juan Capistrano Fountains" src="https://hotmamatravel.com/wp-content/uploads/2015/09/SanJuan1-1024x512.jpg" alt="Mission San Juan Capistrano Fountains" width="640" height="320" /></a>
<h3><span style="color: #000000;">Ruins of The Great Stone Church</span></h3>
<span style="color: #000000;">My favorite part of Mission San Juan Capistrano is the old church ruins. It is jaw dropping. Once known as the Great Stone Church, it was built in 1806 and was one of the largest structures in Alta California. Sadly, an earthquake struck during Mass in 1812 and the church crumbled, killing 40 people! What remains is a giant reminder of this tragedy that happened so long ago. I can’t get enough of it when I visit. I try to imagine how glorious it must have looked in 1806, because even now, in ruins, the Great Stone Church is magnificent.</span>

<a href="/mission-san-juan-capistrano/" rel="attachment wp-att-5425"><img class="aligncenter wp-image-5425" title="Ruins at Mission San Juan Capistrano" src="https://hotmamatravel.com/wp-content/uploads/2015/09/sanjuan3-1024x791.jpg" alt="Ruins at Mission San Juan Capistrano" width="640" height="494" /></a>
<span style="color: #000000;">Come on St. Joseph’s Day (March 19th) and see the witness the miracle of the Swallows. The little swallows flock to the San Juan Capistrano to build their nests atop the ruins of the Great Stone Church. Apparently, it is quite a spectacle. On October 23rd they leave, but not before circling the ruins a few times, as if to say goodbye.</span>
<h3><span style="color: #000000;">Living Quarters</span></h3>
<span style="color: #000000;">Spend the rest of the day exploring the property. Checkout the soldiers’ quarters and be sure to listen to the free audio tour to hear interesting tales of pirate and soldier encounters.</span>

<a href="/mission-san-juan-capistrano/" rel="attachment wp-att-5427"><img class="aligncenter wp-image-5427" title="Inside Mission San Juan Capistrano" src="https://hotmamatravel.com/wp-content/uploads/2015/09/sanjuan5-1024x672.jpg" alt="Inside Mission San Juan Capistrano" width="640" height="420" /></a>

<span style="color: #000000;">You can even explore the rooms of the Padres. Such humble dwellings, you’ll learn about the modern urban luxuries of Mexico the Padres had to give up in order to serve at the missions of Alta California.</span>

<a href="/mission-san-juan-capistrano/"><img class="aligncenter wp-image-3293" title="Kitchen inside San Juan Capistrano" src="https://hotmamatravel.com/wp-content/uploads/2015/09/DSCN0828-1024x768.jpg" alt="Kitchen inside San Juan Capistrano" width="640" height="480" /></a>
<h3><span style="color: #000000;">Natives</span></h3>
<span style="color: #000000;">My kids really enjoyed learning about the Native Californians. FireCracker, was really intrigued by the cooking methods of the women and kitchen at the mission. My son, CuddleBear, enjoyed playing with the musical instruments.</span>
<h2><span style="color: #000000;"><a href="/mission-san-juan-capistrano/" rel="attachment wp-att-5443"><img class="aligncenter wp-image-5443" title="Kids Activity Mission San Juan Capistrano" src="https://hotmamatravel.com/wp-content/uploads/2015/09/SJ.jpg" alt="Kids Activity Mission San Juan Capistrano" width="640" height="640" /></a></span></h2>
<h3><span style="color: #000000;">Serra Chapel</span></h3>
<span style="color: #000000;">Named after the founder of the Mission, St. Junipero Serra, this beautiful chapel is known for its beautiful and ornate baroque alter. That alter came from Barcelona, Spain is said to be over 400 years old!  Off to the side of the entrance is St. Peregrine Chapel, which is a little prayer room dedicated to the patron saint of suffers. The kids really enjoyed visiting the chapel where they made a prayer for members of our family while lighting a candle.
</span>

<a href="/mission-san-juan-capistrano/" rel="attachment wp-att-5442"><img class="aligncenter wp-image-5442" title="Serra Chapel Mission San Juan Capistrano" src="https://hotmamatravel.com/wp-content/uploads/2015/09/SJMCv2.jpg" alt="Serra Chapel Mission San Juan Capistrano" width="640" height="723" /></a>
<h2><span style="color: #000000;"><em><strong>Places to Grab a Drink:</strong></em></span></h2>
<span style="color: #000000;">There great restaurants in close proximity to Mission San Juan Capistrano, many of which support the mission. Two of my favorite restaurants, also supporters, are El Adobe and Cedar Creek.</span>
<h3><span style="color: #000000;">El Adobe</span></h3>
<span style="color: #000000;">While I was an intern at Churm Media, I reviewed this restaurant. I love the atmosphere and the yummy Mexican cuisine. It is walking distance from the mission and was even on an episode of Real Housewives of Orange County.</span>
<h3><span style="color: #000000;"><strong>Cedar Creek</strong> </span></h3>
<span style="color: #000000;">It not only has delicious food, but amazing views of the Great Stone Church Ruins. I love coming here after a long day exploring Mission San Juan Capistrano, sipping on a crisp glass of Blanc while gazing upon the brilliant site. Cedar Creek also has live music on the patio during the weekends, making it a winner in my book. Hopefully your kids will be nice and tired, so you can enjoy a little wine and dine in peace.</span>

<a href="/mission-san-juan-capistrano/" rel="attachment wp-att-5444"><img class="aligncenter wp-image-5444" title="Cedar Creek Inn Capistrano" src="https://hotmamatravel.com/wp-content/uploads/2015/09/SJC.jpg" alt="Cedar Creek Inn Capistrano" width="640" height="530" /></a>
<h3><span style="color: #000000;">Mission Grill</span></h3>
For lunch, I like to relax at the <strong>Mission Grill</strong><span style="color: #ff0000;"><strong><span style="color: #000000;">.</span></strong></span>It is patio seating, views of the mission and great prices. When prices are low, I can really let my hair down and over-order. Margaritas, cervezas, stuffed jalapenos, carnitas tacos, chorizo burgers, churros and more!! Plus it is nice and casual for the kids.

<a href="/mission-san-juan-capistrano/" rel="attachment wp-att-5445"><img class="aligncenter wp-image-5445" title="Mission Grill Capistrano" src="https://hotmamatravel.com/wp-content/uploads/2015/09/SJC1.jpg" alt="Mission Grill Capistrano" width="640" height="640" /></a>
<h2><span style="color: #000000;"><em><strong>That’s a Wrap:</strong></em></span></h2>
<span style="color: #000000;">If you love visiting historical sites, then Mission San Juan Capistrano is the perfect spot to stop. It is definitely one of the most famous missions in Alta California and the ruins alone make it worth the trip. Luckily, all 21 of Alta California Missions are along California’s gorgeous coast, so after seeing the mission there is lots to explore or if you are feeling adventurous, you could take a tour on the mission trail. Needless to say, the missions are fascinating and although I have visited Mission San Juan Capistrano several times in my life, it never gets old!</span>
