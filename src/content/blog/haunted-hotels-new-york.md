## **[Hotel Chelsea](/go/hotel-chelsea-new-york/)**

222 West Twenty-Third Street New York, N. Y. 10011
</div>

### **Brief History**

Ah, the [Hotel Chelsea](https://hotelchelsea.com/) in New York City! This place oozes history, culture, and, some say, a touch of the supernatural. Imagine a building that's seen more drama than a Shakespearean play and hosted more artists than a hipster's loft party. Well, that's the Chelsea for you.

Built in 1883, this grand old dame started life as a co-op apartment building. But things took a turn when it converted into a hotel in 1905. From then on, it became a haven for artists, writers, and musicians looking for a slice of bohemian paradise. The Chelsea Hotel is the kind of place where you might bump into the likes of Mark Twain, Thomas Wolfe, or Arthur C. Clarke in the elevator (assuming they're ghostwriting their next masterpiece).

The hotel's claim to fame? The "Chelsea Girls" film by Andy Warhol that was partly shot there in the 1960s. Warhol's Factory crowd practically lived there, making the Chelsea a hub for creative chaos and, let's face it, a fair bit of debauchery.

The Chelsea Hotel is like a time capsule of New York's artistic history. Bob Dylan composed songs here, Jack Kerouac typed away, and countless musicians strummed their guitars in smoky rooms. But it's not all roses and sunshine – it's seen its fair share of tragedies and dark moments too.

Despite renovations and changes over the years, the Chelsea still stands proudly, a living (and maybe a little haunted) testament to the bohemian spirit of NYC. So, if you want to soak up some culture, feel the ghosts of creative genius, or just maybe catch a glimpse of a spectral poet scribbling in the corner, the Chelsea Hotel is the place to be.

### **Hauntings**

Well, grab your ghost-hunting gear and your artistic sensibilities because we're about to delve into the spooky and creative world of the Hotel Chelsea in New York! This place isn't just famous for its artistic residents; it's got a reputation for some ghostly company as well.

First up, we've got the spirit of Welsh poet Dylan Thomas. He was known to frequent the Chelsea, and rumor has it that he loved it so much that he never really left. Some folks claim to have heard him whispering poetry in the halls, perhaps seeking inspiration from the afterlife.

Then there's the tale of Nancy Spungen. She's a bit of a punk rock legend due to [her tragic demise](https://faroutmagazine.co.uk/the-chelsea-hotel-tragic-unsolved-death-nancy-spungen/), allegedly at the hands of Sid Vicious of the Sex Pistols. Some believe Nancy's restless spirit lingers in the Chelsea, maybe in the room where she met her unfortunate end.

But wait, there's more! The Chelsea Hotel has so many ghostly stories that you could write a whole book about them. Guests and staff have reported strange noises, flickering lights, and unexplained apparitions. Some have even claimed to witness furniture moving on its own, as if the spirits are redecorating.

It's said that the hotel's eerie ambiance isn't just a result of its famous former residents; it's also due to its unique architecture, which includes long, labyrinthine hallways and creaky old floors. All of this adds to the eerie charm that makes you wonder if you're alone in your room at night.

So, if you're up for a thrilling stay and a dash of artistic inspiration, check in and see if you can make a spectral friend or two. Just don't forget to say "hi" to Dylan and Nancy for us!

## **[The Algonquin Hotel](/go/the-algonquin-hotel-new-york/)**

59 West 44th Street, New York City, NY 10036
</div>

### **Brief History**

The Algonquin Hotel in New York City, where history, elegance, and a dash of wit come together in one delightful cocktail of a building! This place isn't just a hotel; it's a living, breathing piece of New York's literary and cultural history.

Picture this: It's 1902, and New York's theater and literary scenes are buzzing like a beehive in a flower field. That's when the [Algonquin Hotel](https://www.algonquinhotel.com/) opened its doors. It quickly became the hub for the city's creative minds, especially during the early 20th century.

The Algonquin Round Table was where the magic happened. Imagine Dorothy Parker, Robert Benchley, and other sharp-tongued writers and wits gathering for lunch, trading quips like they were currency. These were the original influencers, and their daily meetings at the Round Table were like a precursor to today's Twitter feuds.

Speaking of Dorothy Parker, she's one of the Algonquin's most famous residents. Known for her acerbic wit and sharp humor, she penned many of her most famous works within the hotel's walls. Legend has it that her spirit still lingers, ready to critique your cocktail choices or your choice of words.

Over the years, the Algonquin has hosted countless luminaries, from playwrights and actors to politicians and journalists. It's a place where creativity sparks in every corner, and where you can almost feel the echoes of conversations that shaped literary history.

Now, it's not just the brilliant minds that haunt this place; it's also rumored to have a few friendly ghosts. Guests have reported strange occurrences and mysterious happenings, as if the hotel's past inhabitants are still enjoying their literary discussions.

So, if you find yourself in the heart of Manhattan and you're seeking a dose of history with a side of spirited conversation, the Algonquin Hotel is your place to be. It's where the past meets the present, where words come to life, and where you might just have a witty encounter with a resident ghost or two. Cheers to the Algonquin!

### **Hauntings**

Gather 'round, because the Algonquin Hotel in New York City has its fair share of ghostly tales to tell, and they're the kind that'll make you both shiver and smile.

First off, let's talk about the star of the spectral show: the legendary wit herself, Dorothy Parker. She's rumored to be the unofficial queen of this paranormal party. You see, Dorothy was known for her sharp tongue and clever quips during her heyday at the Algonquin. It seems she couldn't resist the urge to continue her witty banter from beyond the grave. Some guests have claimed to hear her disembodied voice whispering words of wisdom, or perhaps just some well-timed sarcasm.

But Dorothy isn't alone in this spirited soirée. The ghost of a mysterious "Man in a Grey Suit" has been spotted roaming the halls. Nobody knows exactly who this dapper apparition might be, but he sure adds a touch of elegance to the hotel's spectral roster.

Now, don't think the Algonquin's ghosts are all about conversation. Some have reported feeling strange cold spots, flickering lights, and unexplained noises. It's as if the spirits are putting on a variety show to keep guests entertained.

But here's the thing about the Algonquin's hauntings: they're not spooky in a scary way. It's more like the ghosts are continuing the tradition of the Algonquin Round Table, where clever minds gathered for witty repartee. You might even say that these spirits are here for a literary critique or two.

So, if you're checking into the Algonquin Hotel, don't be surprised if you find yourself in a conversation with the supernatural. And remember, if you hear a disembodied voice cracking a clever joke or critiquing your fashion choices, it's probably just Dorothy Parker, keeping the party alive in the afterlife. Cheers to spirited soirées at the Algonquin!

## **[The Sagamore&nbsp;](/go/the-sagamore-resort-new-york/)**

110 Sagamore Rd, Bolton Landing, NY 12814
</div>

### **Brief History**

The story begins in 1883 when hotelier Myron O. Brown opened the doors of the first Sagamore, a grand hotel that quickly became a haven for well-heeled travelers seeking respite in the pristine wilderness. The name "Sagamore" itself is a nod to the Native American term for a tribal chief, hinting at the resort's majestic status.

The Sagamore has seen its fair share of history. It weathered a devastating fire in 1893 but emerged stronger, rebuilt in the charming Victorian style that still captivates guests today. During World War II, the resort transformed into a training ground for the U.S. Navy, hosting thousands of sailors.

Over the decades, it's welcomed famous faces, from presidents like Franklin D. Roosevelt to Hollywood celebrities seeking a slice of lakeside paradise. [The Sagamore Resort](https://www.opalcollection.com/sagamore/) has maintained its reputation as a luxurious retreat. It's the kind of place where you can sip a cocktail on the veranda, soak in Lake George's beauty, and imagine the whispers of history echoing through the halls.

### **Hauntings**

Well, folks, if you ever find yourself wandering the halls of the Sagamore Resort on Lake George in New York, keep an eye out for more than just stunning lake views and cozy fireplaces. You might just stumble upon a ghostly guest or two!

First up, we have "The Smoker," a mysterious spirit who's quite the enigma. This spectral character apparently enjoys puffing on an invisible cigar in the resort's elegant hallways. Now, I've heard of secondhand smoke, but this is taking it to a whole new level!

But The Smoker isn't the resort's only phantom. Some guests have reported encountering shadowy figures and hearing eerie sounds when the moonlight dances on the lake's surface. It's like a supernatural symphony by the water's edge.

Now, let's be clear, the hauntings at the Sagamore Resort aren't of the hair-raising, terrifying variety. It's more like the spirits are just enjoying the resort's luxurious amenities in the afterlife. Who can blame them, right? Imagine being a ghost with eternal access to those lake views!

So, if you decide to check into the Sagamore, don't be surprised if you hear a faint puffing sound in the hallway or glimpse a figure out of the corner of your eye. It's all part of the resort's quirky charm, where the living and the not-so-living can share a lakeside vacation. Just remember to say a polite "hello" to The Smoker if you see him; ghostly manners are important, after all!

## **[The Mohonk Mountain House](/haunted-hotels-new-york/)**

1000 Mountain Rest Rd, New Paltz, NY 12561
</div>

### **Brief History**

Nestled in the picturesque Shawangunk Mountains of upstate New York, the Mohonk Mountain House is a historic resort that's been offering guests a taste of natural beauty and Victorian elegance since its inception in 1869.

The story begins with twin brothers Albert and Alfred Smiley, who had a vision of creating a peaceful retreat amidst the rugged wilderness. They acquired a small inn and gradually expanded it into the grand mountain resort it is today. [The Mohonk Mountain House](https://www.mohonk.com/) was designed in a stunning Victorian style, with turrets, balconies, and verandas that provide breathtaking views of the surrounding landscape.

From its early days, the resort has been a sanctuary for nature lovers and outdoor enthusiasts. Guests could hike, swim, boat, and take in the fresh mountain air. The resort's pristine lake, Lake Mohonk, offered a tranquil escape from the hustle and bustle of city life.

Throughout its history, the Mohonk Mountain House has hosted notable figures, including U.S. Presidents, artists, and writers seeking inspiration from the serene surroundings. It's also known for its commitment to environmental conservation and sustainability, a tradition that began in the late 19th century and continues today.

As for its alleged hauntings, the Mohonk Mountain House has its share of ghostly tales. One of the most famous involves a little girl who tragically drowned in the lake. Some guests claim to have seen her ghostly figure or heard her laughter echoing by the water.

### **Hauntings**

Hold onto your hiking boots because we're about to dive into the delightfully eerie world of the Mohonk Mountain House hauntings!

First up, the ghostly girl of Lake Mohonk. Legend has it that a young girl tragically drowned in the lake many moons ago. But her spirit seems to have decided that her vacation wasn't quite over, as guests have reported seeing her apparition near the water's edge. Some even say they've heard her playful laughter dancing on the wind.

Now, if you're thinking about a relaxing soak in the hotel's charming Victorian-era spa, you might just have a bath buddy from the other side. Guests have reported mysterious appearances of a ghostly figure in the spa area, adding an extra layer of relaxation or perhaps an unexpected conversation starter to your spa day.

But it's not all watery apparitions and spa specters. The Mohonk Mountain House has its fair share of creaks, groans, and inexplicable sounds that could give even the bravest guests a case of the goosebumps. It's as if the old walls have stories to tell, and they're not shy about sharing them.

Now, these ghosts aren't your typical spooky spirits. They seem more like they're just adding a bit of supernatural flair to your mountain getaway. Think of them as the original guests who never quite checked out, adding an extra layer of charm to the already enchanting Mohonk Mountain House.

## **[The Otesaga Resort Hotel](/go/otesaga-resort-new-york/)**

60 Lake St, Cooperstown, Otsego, NY 13326
</div>

### **Brief History**

The [Otesaga Resort Hotel](https://www.otesaga.com/), located in Cooperstown, New York, is a historic and elegant lakeside retreat with a rich history dating back to 1909.

The story begins with the Clark family, who envisioned creating a luxurious resort on the shores of Otsego Lake. They commissioned architect Percy Griffin to design a grand hotel that would complement the area's natural beauty. The result was the Otesaga Resort, a stunning example of Colonial Revival architecture.

From its early days, the Otesaga has been a favorite destination for travelers seeking relaxation and recreation. Its picturesque location near the Baseball Hall of Fame and the Farmers' Museum has made it a popular choice for visitors exploring the cultural and natural attractions of Cooperstown.

Over the years, the Otesaga has hosted numerous notable guests, including celebrities, politicians, and even several U.S. presidents. Its reputation for luxury and hospitality has made it a beloved destination for weddings, conferences, and leisure getaways.

The resort offers a wide range of amenities, including a golf course, a spa, and fine dining options. Its charming lakeside setting and elegant ambiance make it a sought-after destination for those looking to experience the beauty and history of upstate New York.

### **Hauntings**

This charming lakeside retreat, located in Cooperstown, is known not only for its stunning views of Otsego Lake and its rich history but also for a few ghostly guests who seem to have checked in... and never left.

First up, we have the tale of "The Lady in White." Rumor has it that a ghostly woman, clad in a flowing white dress, roams the hallways of the Otesaga. Perhaps she's still searching for her lost love or maybe just enjoying the luxurious accommodations from the great beyond.

Then there's the story of the mysterious "Crying Lady." Some guests have reported hearing sobs echoing through the hallways in the dead of night. Is it the lament of a bygone era or simply the sound of someone who couldn't resist the temptation of the resort's exquisite spa treatments?

But don't let these tales scare you off. The Otesaga's ghostly residents are said to be more playful than spooky. There have been reports of lights flickering, objects moving on their own, and even the sensation of someone gently tucking you in at night. It's as if the spirits want to ensure your stay is nothing short of magical.

So, if you decide to tee off at the Leatherstocking Golf Course or take a leisurely stroll along the lake at the Otesaga Resort, keep an eye out for these friendly apparitions. They're just adding an extra layer of charm to this historic gem in Cooperstown, making your stay a memorable blend of luxury, natural beauty, and the possibility of a playful spectral encounter.

### **Why The Otesaga Resort Is HotMamaTravel Approved**

The Otesaga Resort Hotel is one of those rare stays that makes the whole family happy. Set right on the shores of Lake Otsego, this grand historic resort has that classic “summer retreat” feel—only with all the modern comforts families need. Kids will love the outdoor pool, lake activities like canoeing and kayaking, and plenty of wide-open lawns to run wild. Parents will appreciate the elegant touches, from the lakeside dining to the golf course and spa.
