If you followed us on our 60 day **[road trip](/family-road-trip-across-us/)** across the US last summer (#HotMamaDoesAmerica), you will have noticed that we spend a lot of time in bars, pubs, saloons and basically any place with live music and cocktails. This is just how we roll, and **there is no shame in our game**. And the kids seem to love it too.

[![kids dancing in new orleans to live jazz music Market Cafe](/images/kids-dancing-in-new-orleans-live-music-jazz.gif)](/kid-friendly-travel-blogger-roundup/)

One thing we noticed traveling from city to city, is just **how few resources there are on the web** for finding "kid friendly" establishments that serve great drinks and live music. We would often have to call each place to find out what time the music started and whether or not they allowed kids in the bar area.

As parents, we have decided to give our children relatively uncensored experiences of cities and towns everywhere. This can mean different things depending on the city, for example visiting a** [prisoner of war memorial](/visiting-andersonville-prison-kids/)** in Georgia, the **[ruins of a historic haunted prison](/things-to-do-in-philadelphia-with-kids/)** in Philadelphia, a [**medieval torture chamber museum**](/edinburgh-castle-with-kids/) in Scotland, **[mine tours](/tombstone-with-kids/)** in Tombstone, family-friendly **[hotels in Vegas](/best-family-hotels-in-las-vegas/)** and various bars and saloons from **[Scottsdale](/best-western-sundial/)** to **[Memphis](/one-day-in-memphis/)**.

**So kid friendly for us quite literally means that the kids are simply allowed in the door.**

We started to wonder why there are so few resources on the web for families like ours. **Are we the only parents looking for this kind of family fun!?** In fact, I did some keyword research and found that while thousands of parents search each month for kid friendly resorts, recipes and casseroles, less than a hundred are searching for kid friendly pubs, live music and dancing.

**You may not agree, but I think there is something wrong with this picture.**

To find out if we are alone in this, **I decided to ask 14 of the best family travel bloggers** with expertise in traveling the world with kids. I basically asked these two questions:

 	- *What makes a place kid friendly and how often do you seek out kid friendly experiences when you travel?*

 	- *Does a place have to be kid-oriented to be considered kid friendly?*

To get a summary of the results at a glance, **we have created this custom, shareable infographic** that has unique data and selected quotes from each of the great travel bloggers who volunteered to contribute to this article. *Click the image to see it in full resolution, and be sure to continue below for  full answers from each contributor.*

"Family friendly is not checking off a label, it's an attitude and an atmosphere. It's being welcoming and respectful towards children. It's seeing them as a present participant in the travel experience, not a side event. It's an experience that helps them learn, interact, and have fun. It doesn't have to be kid-oriented, but can be kid-friendly simply with the right attitude.

The first thing we check when seeking out travel related experiences or attractions is age appropriateness. It doesn't have to be necessarily kid-friendly, as we can generally have a wonderful time even when things aren't necessarily kid friendly. But, if it's not appropriate for their age, then we don't visit.

We're getting ready to go to New Orleans for Mardi Gras, which many people have warned us it's not kid friendly and not to go. I don't agree. I think you can definitely seek out the family friendly experiences - children live in New Orleans so they'll be there. Sure, we won't be throwing beads on Bourbon Street, but we'll find some neighbourhoods that cater to the kids and have a wonderful time. I don't agree with shielding our children from experiencing incredibly cultural celebrations and festivals because of a few people behaving badly. They can still get the spirit and the atmosphere from those who behave well and welcome the children as part of the experience.

Some things are not kid friendly because they may be too young to understand or too small enough to manage it. If that is the case, we often will tag team with each other. One will do the adult friendly activity while the other takes the kids to a child-friendly experience, like a kid's museum. We have a four year age gap with our girls so sometimes our youngest can't do an activity, so we split up again.

I don't think hotels matter so much. As long as there is a bed they can sleep in, we're not too fussed at how kid-friendly it is as for us it's just a place to sleep. We rarely stay at resorts, but if we do, we'd look for swimming pools - ones with water slides work well, kid's club and water sport activities. We stayed at a **[fabulous family friendly resort on Boracay Island](https://www.ytravelblog.com/movenpick-resort-review-boracay-island/) **that had all of these facilities, including cooking lessons, hair braiding, chocolate tasting and kayaks and stand up paddle boards for use on the private beach. Now that was magic and worth seeking out. I think any attraction that helps keeps the kids happy and engaged so that parents can relax and also enjoy the vacation is winning.

For restaurants, we definitely seek out kid friendly menus and atmosphere. We love restaurants that have activity packs or things for the kids to do while sitting at the table. Anything with live music works well. some restaurants will have a kid play section or outside area where they can run around or climb on play equipment. These restaurants are always packed with happy kids and parents. Australian restaurants do this really well.

We spent some time recently in Nashville with the kids and visited many adult-friendly music establishments with the kids. Some of the ways they made it kid friendly was by having certain hours of the day for families or certain nights that were family nights. We had an evening at the Bluebird Cafe for open mic. Kids usually don't go, but ours were welcomed. They had a kids menu and the artists interacted with the girls and high fived them as they walked past. This was a friendly attitude which makes kids feel wonderful. My girls sat quietly for the two hour show and loved it! The Grand Ole Opry was another event that catered to kids and adults. Sometimes popcorn, ice cream, and an entertaining show is enough for the kids. The Grand Ole Opry was more kid friendly than the Bluebird as it was a loud show so kids could be a bit more fidgety and were encouraged to sing along with the performers.

Other attractions can be great at offering kid version audio tours. We had this experience at the **[Daintree Rainforest Center in Australia](https://www.ytravelblog.com/the-daintree-rainforest/)**. It would have been very boring for the girls otherwise, but the audio kept them engaged and they had scavenger hunt tasks to do. I think age appropriate ways to engage in an adult friendly activity like this can work really well."

"Being "kid-friendly" doesn't mean that that a place or experience is designed for kids, but that it is appropriate for and even encourages kids to be a part of it. A kid friendly destination is a place where kids will both be welcome and be able to enjoy themselves in some capacity. There needs to be something that's engaging in a non-academic way and probably also visually stimulating to keep a kid's attention.

We do fully consider how kid-friendly a place is going be before confirming travel plans. When it comes to hotels, we don't care about the style as long as we have enough space in our room for the four of us to function (we spend very little time in our hotel rooms). For restaurants, ideally a kid-friendly restaurant would have a menu that reflects a variety of foods that adults would eat too, but child sized. When dining out, there's nothing more frustrating to us as parents than seeing that, yet again, our kids can have mac 'n cheese or a grilled cheese sandwich. Do restaurants think kids can't eat human food? And when we're talking about attractions and events, we try to incorporate our kids into almost everything, so they are just as comfortable visiting a museum or historic site as they are watching a parade in Disneyland. You'd be hard pressed to find an attraction/site that didn't have some aspect of kid-friendliness.

When we're visiting a place that's clearly meant for adults, we bring along some sort of activity for the kids. If we're doing something relating to culture or history, we take the time to explain what we're doing and we talk to the kids about it before we even get there. And something really cool that many people might not know is that adult activities, such as wine tasting, usually have some sort of experience for kids too, whether it's a play area, a snack tasting, or even grapes to crush. Businesses are getting wise to the fact that parents take their kids everywhere nowadays."

"Kid-friendly simply means that something is able to be enjoyed by my kids. It definitely doesn’t need to be kid-oriented. Some of our favorite hotels around the world aren’t kid-oriented, but they’re kid-friendly because our kids are comfortable there. The employees go out of their way to make my kids feel welcome. They’re proactive at suggesting things that we would enjoy.

Kid-friendliness is important when we’re pre-booking tours or activities. Are they planned with kids in mind? Will my kids find them interesting? If it’s a cooking class, do they even want kids there? A four-hour tour of a museum or a European city is not kid-friendly, but a 1.5-hour tour with a scavenger hunt is.
But kid-friendliness isn’t important when we are seeking out hotels. I want to stay at the perfect hotel in the perfect location anywhere we travel – high-end or low-end – and that’s rarely going to be a kid-oriented hotel. A boutique hotel in Florence can be kid-friendly. The Park Hyatt Tokyo is my 7-year-old daughter’s favorite hotel anywhere. It’s not kid-oriented, but we felt incredibly welcome and comfortable there. The Four Seasons Bora Bora and Six Senses Laamu in the Maldives cater far more to honeymooners than to families, but both were very kid-friendly.

The problem is that you don’t always know what’s kid-friendly or not kid-friendly until you go. So we stay where we want to stay and hope for the best, and we’ve only been disappointed in kid-friendliness a handful of times. Likewise, we dine where we want based on the foodie tastes of our kids. We don’t need crayons. Our kids are well-behaved. In regards to hotels and restaurants, we figure as long as they allow our children in the door that is friendly enough for us. While we don’t necessarily base many parts of our travels on kid-friendliness, I admit that planning for our itinerary is largely based on attractions and activities we believe our kids would have a fun time participating in. As I am filling our schedule I make a conscious effort to include stops in-between “grown-up” activities that are age appropriate and fun for them. For example, in every country we have traveled to thus far, we have made a conscious effort to spent time in playgrounds, botanical gardens, aquariums, zoos, theme parks and taking hikes in nature.

I believe many adult friendly activities can be catered to kids as the parent takes into account what he/she thinks their kids can handle and make safety a top priority. One example is when we visited the coastal region of Lima Peru where we discovered paragliding at the edge of the cliffs along the seashore. I would have never considered taking a leap off a cliff strapped to a parachute at the mercy of the wind to be a kid-friendly activity. However, we were surprised to find out the youngest age of participants to be allowed was 5 years old when we inquired. Our son who at the time was 7 years old was so excited to try it, and after much pondering we agreed to let him do it. In the end he had a great time and he still talks about what an epic time he had peacefully gliding through the air while admiring the amazing views of the coast below."

"Kid-friendly generally means that kids are allowed in the door for my family. No one will stare at us or make rude sounds when I sit down or wander through with my troop. Kid-oriented is when a destination, attraction or restaurant is actually geared towards children. These spots have a time and a place for sure, but they aren’t the spots I like to center our trips around.

I don’t look for the typical meaning of “kid-friendly” at all when I plan our travels. My kids can find fun anywhere because I have always planned what we want to see as a family first. I don’t believe in sitting on my phone while my kids experience a destination. I’m not paying all of this money for anyone to be bored for long periods of time. I want us to interact together and have an adventure. That means I look for the total package when it comes to activities. Yes, I need to make sure my children are allowed through the doors. Obviously, if children under 12 are not allowed to stay at a B&amp;B or you have to be 18+ to enter a restaurant, we aren’t going to make a reservation. I’m not going to avoid the cute boutique hotels and a great location just because it doesn’t have a playground next door or an indoor pool for my kids though.

Kid-friendly comes in so many shapes and sizes. Simply having crayons at a restaurant can make a place kid-friendly, but you know what, as a mom, my purse is what makes a place kid-friendly. I always have an activity ready to go while we wait for a meal. Activities that are hands-on or have scavenger hunt sheets and audio guides also make a spot kid-friendly. Some of the top museums in the world, ones that many parents would never think to bring their young children, actually have some of the best children’s programming. I’ve had to drag my 5-year-old son out of Holyrood Palace because I got bored well before he did. He was that engrossed in the children’s audio guide they had created. Obviously I should have asked for the kid’s guide instead of the adult guide. The lesson learned here— never underestimate what your children will enjoy. Kids don’t need clowns, cartoons and screens to have fun. They can find fun anywhere, especially if they can explore with their family and share the magic they find in the world."

*Thank you Keryn!*
