<p class="has-drop-cap">Few highways capture the American imagination like Route 66, the legendary <em>Mother Road</em> that has inspired generations of road trippers chasing nostalgia, adventure, and iconic roadside charm.</p>
<!-- /wp:paragraph -->

<!-- wp:paragraph -->
<p>As a family that’s lived out West our entire lives, we were inspired by "<strong><a href="https://amzn.to/2MOKNQk" target="_blank" rel="noreferrer noopener">The Best Hits on Route 66: 100 Essential Stops on the Mother Road</a></strong>" by Amy Bizzarri, and decided to flip the classic journey and take our kids on a Route 66 family road trip itinerary, traveling <em>eastbound</em> from Los Angeles to Santa Fe. </p>
<!-- /wp:paragraph -->

<!-- wp:paragraph -->
<p>Our goal? Explore quirky roadside attractions, historic diners, neon-lit motels, and unforgettable landmarks that make this stretch of Route 66 one of the most fun and family-friendly road trips in the U.S.</p>
<!-- /wp:paragraph -->

<!-- wp:paragraph -->
<p>Along the way, we stayed at <strong><a href="/best-western-family-travel/" data-type="post" data-id="11422" target="_blank" rel="noreferrer noopener">Best Western® Hotels &amp; Resorts</a></strong> the entire trip, a reliable, affordable, and family-friendly hotel choice that made this multi-city adventure easier, more comfortable, and stress-free for parents traveling with kids.</p>
<!-- /wp:paragraph -->

<!-- wp:paragraph -->
<p>If you love road trips, you may also enjoy our <strong><a href="/family-road-trip-across-us/" data-type="post" data-id="7132" target="_blank" rel="noreferrer noopener">cross-country family road trip adventure</a></strong> , where we covered even more iconic American routes and destinations.</p>
<!-- /wp:paragraph -->

<!-- wp:paragraph -->
<p>Below, you’ll find our easy Route 66 family road trip itinerary, including the best stops, kid-friendly highlights, must-see attractions, and travel tips for each city, plus why this trip is perfect for families who want adventure without overcomplicating the drive.</p>
<!-- /wp:paragraph -->

<h3>Day 1, Stop 1 (passing through)</h3>
<!-- /wp:heading -->

<!-- wp:paragraph -->
<p>Once upon a time, Oatman was a main gold producer and was chock full 'o prospectors trying to strike it rich. Now it is a historic ghost town full of...wild burros!? </p>
<!-- /wp:paragraph -->

<!-- wp:paragraph -->
<p>Yes, you read that right, there are (mostly) friendly little jackasses roaming the streets of Oatman daily. </p>
<!-- /wp:paragraph -->

<!-- wp:paragraph -->
<p>We love visiting Old West ghost towns like <strong><a href="/tombstone-with-kids/" target="_blank" rel="noreferrer noopener">Tombstone</a> </strong>and <strong><a href="/calico-ghost-town-with-kids/" target="_blank" rel="noreferrer noopener">Calico</a></strong>, and hearing all the fascinating stories. I expected Oatman will fit the bill just perfectly.</p>

<h3>Days 1-2 (stay 2 nights)</h3>
<!-- /wp:heading -->

<!-- wp:paragraph -->
<p>Williams has long been considered the gateway to the Grand Canyon, one of the true wonders of the world. We took a train tour on the historic&nbsp;Grand Canyon Railway from Williams Depot, which made its first journey into the Canyon way back in 1901 (and also doubles as the <strong><a href="/polar-express-in-arizona/" target="_blank" rel="noreferrer noopener">Polar Express</a></strong>)! The quaint but rather bustling town in the high desert is also home to numerous shops and fun places to eat.</p>

<h3>Day 3 (passing through)</h3>
<!-- /wp:heading -->

<!-- wp:paragraph -->
<p>Gallup has served as the backdrop for over 100 old Western Movies over the years, predominantly through the 1930's and 40's. A jewel of this sleepy old town on Route 66 is the <a href="https://elranchohotelgallup.com/" target="_blank" rel="noreferrer noopener">El Rancho Hotel</a>, which was built in 1936 to serve the burgeoning film industry around Gallup. All we did was grab lunch and a drink at El Rancho's 49ers Lounge and take a tour of rustic El Rancho Hotel.</p>

<h3>Day 6 (passing through)</h3>
<!-- /wp:heading -->

<!-- wp:paragraph -->
<p>On our way back from New Mexico, we made a must-stop detour to Holbrook, Arizona, one of the most iconic and nostalgic towns along Route 66. We grabbed lunch at a classic family-owned diner, Joe &amp; Aggies Diner, soaking in the old-school charm and warm hospitality that make small-town stops so special on a road trip. </p>
<!-- /wp:paragraph -->

<!-- wp:paragraph -->
<p>Sadly, the diner, which had been open since 1943, closed in 2020 following the owner's passing.</p>
<!-- /wp:paragraph -->

<!-- wp:paragraph -->
<p>The highlight of our visit was exploring the legendary <strong><a href="https://sleepinawigwam.com/" target="_blank" rel="noreferrer noopener">Wigwam Motel</a></strong>, the <a href="https://sleepinawigwam.com/press-exposure/" target="_blank" rel="noreferrer noopener">inspiration behind the Disney movie, "Cars,"</a> where you can see (or stay in!) teepee-shaped rooms that feel like stepping straight into a piece of Americana. It was the perfect mix of history, kitsch, and family fun, exactly what a Route 66 road trip is all about.</p>
