Broken Bow, Oklahoma is a beautiful and scenic town located in the southeastern part of the state. Known as the gateway to Beavers Bend State Park, Broken Bow has lush forests and clear lakes, it is a popular destination for outdoor enthusiasts and nature lovers. To help you plan your next family escape, here are a few things to do in Broken Bow, Oklahoma.

#### [Beavers Bend State Park](/go/beavers-bend-state-park-broken-bow-oklahoma/)

Beavers Bend State Park in Oklahoma offers a variety of activities and events for families and kids to enjoy. Throughout the year Beaver's Bend hosts a number of exciting events the whole family will love, including Beavers Bend Folk Festival &amp; Craft Show and the Kiamichi Owa-Chito Festival of the Forest. These events feature workshops, live music, activities for the kids, food vendors and more.

The Nature Center not only hosts fun events throughout the year too, like Easter Egg hunts, holiday events, first week events and more, but it is also a great place to take the kids to learn about local flora and fauna, participate in educational programs and interactive exhibits.

For active families, there are a number of trails suited for every skill level. Some of these trails even have playgrounds and picnic areas for families to take a break and enjoy the outdoors. There are also bike trails, as well as horseback-riding opportunities, ATV rentals and golf available. If you want to get out on the water, you can go fishing, canoeing and kayaking.

#### [Broken Bow Lake](/go/broken-bow-lake-broken-bow-oklahoma/)

Surrounded by Hochatown State Park, Broken Bow Lake is a great location for families to spend the day on the water. At Broken Bow Lake you can  enjoy various activities, like swimming area, complete with playground and beach. The lake offers a variety of fishing opportunities for families, including a fishing dock and a catch-and-release pond. You could rent a boat and take family for a day on the lake or to partake in a variety of water sports, including jet skiing and wakeboarding.

The lake area is home to a variety of wildlife, including deer, raccoons, and squirrels. Visitors can take a drive through the area to spot different animals or enjoy the many hiking trails great for all skill levels, including kids.

* 10216 N US Hwy 259, Hochatown, OK 74728*

Girls Gone Wine is a winery located in Broken Bow, Oklahoma that offers a unique and delicious experience for visitors. Owned and operated by a group of friends, the winery is known for producing wines made from locally grown fruits and berries, including blackberries, blueberries, and raspberries, which gives the wines a unique and delicious flavor. The winery also offers a variety of events throughout the year, including live music, wine and food pairing events, and special tastings. These events are a great opportunity to try new wines and mingle with other wine enthusiasts.

Girls Gone Wine is a great place to visit for anyone who loves wine and the outdoors. The winery's beautiful setting, delicious wines, and friendly staff make it a perfect destination for a day trip or a weekend getaway.

You can also purchase wine-related items in the gift shop, such as wine glasses, corkscrews, and other accessories. Children are welcome in our gift shop and south patio with adult supervision.

#### [The Museum of the Red River](/go/museum-red-river-idabel-oklahoma/)

* 812 E Lincoln Rd, Idabel, OK 74745*

The Museum of the Red River in Idabel, Oklahoma is a fascinating and educational destination that offers a glimpse into the history and culture of the region. The museum features exhibits on the area's natural history, Native American artifacts, and local art. The museum is a great place to visit for families, history buffs, and anyone interested in learning more about the region.

The museum's permanent collection includes over 100,000 artifacts and specimens that tell the story of the natural and cultural history of the region. Visitors can explore exhibits on the area's geology, paleontology, and archaeology, as well as learn about the lives of the area's early inhabitants.

The museum's Native American collection is particularly noteworthy, featuring artifacts from the Choctaw, Chickasaw, and Caddo tribes that once inhabited the region. Visitors can see examples of traditional pottery, baskets, and clothing, as well as learn about the tribes' customs and beliefs.

The museum also offers a variety of educational programs for visitors of all ages, including guided tours, lectures, and workshops. These programs are a great way to learn more about the museum's collections and the history of the region.

The Museum of the Red River is a great destination for families, offering interactive exhibits and hands-on educational activities that are perfect for kids. The museum is open Monday through Saturday, and admission is affordable.

#### [Forest Heritage Center Museum](/go/forest-heritage-center-museum-broken-bow-oklahoma/)

* North US Highway 259, Broken Bow, OK 74728*

Forest Heritage Center Museum is a must-see destination for anyone interested in the history of forestry and logging. With its engaging exhibits and interactive displays, it is a great place to learn about the past, present, and future of one of the most important industries in the region.

The museum's exhibits showcase the tools and equipment used by early loggers, including crosscut saws, axes, and two-man saws. You can also learn about the different types of trees found in the region and the importance of sustainable forestry practices.

One of the highlights of the museum is the working sawmill exhibit, which demonstrates the process of sawing logs into lumber. Watch as a log is transformed into planks of wood, then learn about the different grades of lumber.

The museum also features a collection of photographs and artifacts that tell the story of the people who worked in the forest industry, including loggers, sawyers, and mill workers. Visitors can learn about the challenges and dangers faced by these early pioneers and the impact of the forest industry on their lives.

Another interesting exhibit is the simulation of the logging camp, that shows how loggers lived, worked and ate in the early days.

The Forest Heritage Center Museum offers educational programs for school groups, and also provides guided tours of the exhibits. It's best to check the museum's website or call for more information about the hours of operation, admission prices, and special events.

Located in the heart of the great outdoors, this tour offers visitors the chance to soar through the treetops and take in the breathtaking beauty of the surrounding wilderness.
