<h3><span style="color: #ff0000;"><span style="color: #000000;">Independence Hall</span></span></h3>
<span style="color: #000000;">While in town we definitely wanted to visit the many historical places in Philadelphia. <a href="https://www.nps.gov/inde/planyourvisit/independencehall.htm" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer"><span style="color: #ff0000;"><strong>Independence Hall</strong></span></a> is one of the most important places to visit in Philadelphia. This is the site where the United States Declaration of Independence as well as the United States Constitution were debated and approved. It is essentially the location of the birthplace of America and that alone makes it one of the top things to do in Philadelphia.</span>

<span style="color: #000000;">The Independence Hall is a National Historic Site and does not charge admission. Since it is so significant as well as one of the free activities in Philadelphia, it tends to be very popular among visitors. Don’t worry it doesn’t get too crowded, because they only take people in assigned groups with free tickets. I recommend the first thing to do the morning of your visit is to stop by the Independence Visitor Center to get your ticket before the crowds, which tells you what time to return. It is like a fast pass for one of the top tourist attractions in Philadelphia. Without waiting in long queues or spending a ton of cash, Independence Hall is a very easy, educational and fun thing to in Philadelphia with kids.</span>

<span style="color: #000000;">For more fun for kids in Philly, you will often find reenactments going on in the court yard of the Independence Hall. My kids enjoyed watching the show and meeting the actors.</span>

<h3><span style="color: #000000;">Liberty Bell</span></h3>
<span style="color: #000000;">The <span style="color: #ff0000;"><strong><a href="https://www.nps.gov/inde/learn/historyculture/stories-libertybell.htm" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer"><span style="color: #ff0000;">Liberty Bell</span></a></strong></span> was one of the historical sites in Philadelphia that my daughter was most excited to visit. She had just learned about it in school and couldn’t wait to see in person. She also wanted me to send a photo of her with the Liberty Bell to her teacher, which I did. Like Independence Hall it is one those free Philadelphia things to do while in town. However, you do not need a timed ticket to view the Liberty Bell. You just need to wait in line. I was pregnant and Papa has mobility issues and we all found the line fairly easy to handle.</span>

<span style="color: #000000;">Once inside, you wander through a visual timeline telling the story of the Liberty Bell. Then you reach the grand finale. However, the Liberty Bell cracked after it was first rung upon its arrival to Philadelphia. Now, it sits in the Liberty Bell Center for visitors to observe up close and personal.</span>

<span style="color: #000000;">Why is it one of the cool things to do in Philadelphia? For one, it is said to be the most haunted prison in America. Secondly, it opened in 1829 and is considered the world’s first true penitentiary. Lastly, my dad actually performed for the prisoners here as a child in 1940.</span>

<span style="color: #000000;">I was so excited to explore the Eastern State Penitentiary and even more excited to do it with my dad and kids. We had passes thanks to Visit Philly, but at the time of our visit both my kids were under the age of 7. They don’t charge for kids under 7, but they have strict rules.</span>
